# BriefRelay 48-Hour Lead Leak Audit — Sample

> **Synthetic example only.** The businesses, people, inquiries, dates, and outcomes below are fictional. This sample is not evidence of customer results.

## Audit snapshot

- **Business type:** HVAC contractor
- **Input reviewed:** 25 fictional inquiry records
- **Review window:** 14 days
- **Potentially recoverable:** 6
- **Most common leak:** No second follow-up after the first reply

## Ranked missed and recoverable opportunities

| Rank | Synthetic inquiry | Signal | Gap found | Recommended next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jordan — AC stopped cooling | Same-day need; provided ZIP code | First reply sent 19 hours later; no follow-up | Call now, then send availability question |
| 2 | Priya — Replace 18-year-old system | High-intent replacement request | Estimate details sent; no check-in for 6 days | Ask whether she wants two install-window options |
| 3 | Mateo — Mini-split for garage | Scope and photos supplied | Asked for one missing measurement; conversation stopped | Make the measurement request easier and offer a quick call |
| 4 | Erin — Maintenance for two units | Clear service request | Acknowledged but never offered times | Offer two appointment windows |
| 5 | Sam — Intermittent furnace shutdown | Safety-sensitive symptom | Generic reply did not clarify urgency | Ask a safety-screening question and route for owner review |

## Five follow-up drafts for approval

**1. No-cooling request**

> Hi Jordan — checking back on your no-cooling request. Is the system still down, and is someone available today if our team has an opening? If you send the service address, we can review the next step. No appointment is confirmed until our team replies.

**2. Replacement estimate**

> Hi Priya — I wanted to follow up on the replacement information we sent. Would it help if we shared two possible installation windows for you to compare? We can confirm pricing and availability before anything is scheduled.

**3. Mini-split inquiry**

> Hi Mateo — we can keep this moving with one measurement: the approximate width and height of the garage. A rough number is fine for the first review. If easier, reply with a good time for a short call.

**4. Maintenance request**

> Hi Erin — thanks for asking about maintenance for both units. Would a morning or afternoon visit generally work better? Our team will confirm the exact day and service details before booking.

**5. Furnace concern**

> Hi Sam — is the furnace currently running, and have you noticed any gas smell, smoke, or carbon-monoxide alarm? If yes, leave the area and contact emergency services or your gas utility. Otherwise, reply with the best callback number so our team can review the service need. This message does not confirm dispatch.

## Response-gap and lead-leak findings

1. **Slow first response:** 4 of 25 fictional inquiries waited more than 4 business hours.
2. **No second touch:** 8 received one reply but no follow-up within 2 business days.
3. **No clear next step:** 5 replies acknowledged the inquiry without asking a question or offering an action.
4. **High-intent requests blended with routine messages:** Replacement and urgent repair requests were not visibly prioritized.
5. **Safety language needs review:** One furnace draft needed a clear escalation path without diagnosing or promising dispatch.

## 15-minute findings call agenda

1. Confirm which opportunities are still open.
2. Approve, edit, or reject the five drafts.
3. Assign an owner and deadline to each recovery action.
4. Decide whether the recurring leak is process, staffing, or visibility.
5. If useful, review the optional Daily Rescue Brief; no ongoing service is required.

## Safety and approval note

These drafts are starting points, not customer messages already sent. The business must review facts, availability, pricing, safety language, and service promises before approving any outreach. BriefRelay does not diagnose equipment, book work, or send messages automatically as part of the audit.
