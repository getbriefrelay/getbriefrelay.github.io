Vega's Design Bar

Vega owns final design direction. Mercury owns campaign strategy and copy, but public visuals should meet this bar before posting.

Fun and friendly must be visible before anyone reads the caption.
Use bold color, strong contrast, and a real visual idea. Plain text on a nice background fails.
The joke is inbox chaos, missed calls, and lead hiding spots. Never mock owners, customers, or service work.
The brand should feel trustworthy enough for revenue, but too memorable to be mistaken for a status deck.

Core Palette

Color should feel loud enough for social, but grounded enough for a service-business owner to trust.

Desk green #102A2D - authority, dark fields, contrast
Signal teal #24B3A7 - lead paths, rescue moments, status
Warm coral #F46D4F - urgency, missed-lead heat, CTAs
Signal yellow #F2B84B - humor beats, spotlight, badges
Paper white #F7F1E6 - cards, backgrounds, breathing room
Ink #17201D - copy on light surfaces

Typography

Use practical system sans typography. The attitude comes from weight, contrast, and composition, not novelty fonts.

The Inbox Bermuda Triangle
Where good leads go to wear fake mustaches.
Short supporting copy only. If a social graphic needs a paragraph to explain the joke, redesign the graphic.

Layout Rules

Every piece needs a dominant object: an urgent lead card, owner brief, approval lock, voicemail slip, dispatch note, or inbox map.

Social One big idea per square. Use one punchy headline, one strong object, and one small trust cue. Do not fill the canvas with explanation.
Email Funny hook, useful proof. Open with a missed-lead image or joke, then quickly show the operational benefit and approval control.
Board Readable but not sterile. Status pages can be organized and still use the palette, badges, strong section headers, and friendly wording.
Ads Make the problem visible. Show the hiding spot, not abstract AI. Contact forms, voicemail, email threads, and late replies are the villains.

Approval Checklist

Use this before updating Gmail, Instagram, the board, or campaign collateral.

Do

  • Make the missed-lead problem visually obvious in two seconds.
  • Use buyer-safe humor and operational proof in the same piece.
  • Show a concrete artifact: lead card, approval lock, owner brief, voicemail, or inbox map.
  • Keep colors bold, layouts intentional, and text short.

Do Not

  • Ship bland SaaS gradients, stock people, fake AI glow, or generic dashboards.
  • Make the owner or customer the joke.
  • Use a plain quote-card composition for campaign posts.
  • Post anything that looks basic just because the copy is good.
